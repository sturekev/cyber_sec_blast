## Keys Without Trust Assumptions: QKD + PQC for the Quantum Era

What happens to today’s secure network sessions when quantum computers can
challenge the public-key cryptography used to establish their keys?

My Computer Networks I research project explores **Quantum Key Distribution
(QKD)** and **Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC)** as a networking problem—not
just a cryptography problem.

The key takeaway: generating quantum keys is only one part of the challenge.
They must be authenticated, delivered to applications, and integrated into
protocols such as IKEv2. Meanwhile, real deployments must manage key rate,
latency, key-pool availability, network reach, and trusted relay nodes.

The practical direction is hybrid: use PQC for scalable protection and
authentication, and consider QKD as an additional key source on selected
high-value links where the infrastructure makes sense.

QKD does not eliminate trust or replace the Internet. It changes where trust
resides—and that makes network architecture and operations central to the
discussion.
